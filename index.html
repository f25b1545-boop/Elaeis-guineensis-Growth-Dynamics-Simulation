Application of Derivatives in Modeling Trunk Height Growth of Elaeis guineensis in Malaysian Plantation Systems

Location Context: Malaysia (MPOB Guidelines)

Model Parameters: Tenera Cultivar

Mathematical Biology of Oil Palm Growth

In commercial Malaysian agriculture, the vertical trunk growth dynamic of Elaeis guineensis (Tenera genotype) represents a critical physical variable. Trunk height ($H$) dictates harvesting labor costs, light capture efficiency, and ultimate plantation replanting life-cycles (typically 25-30 years). Using standard empirical growth data compiled across Peninsular and East Malaysian plantations, we map trunk elongation through a sigmoid Logistic Growth Function.

Primary Height Function $H(t)$
$$H(t) = \frac{12}{1 + 5e^{-0.22t}}$$

Where $H(t)$ is height in meters, and $t$ is palm age in years since field planting.

First Derivative $H'(t)$ (Velocity)
$$H'(t) = \frac{13.2 e^{-0.22t}}{(1 + 5e^{-0.22t})^2}$$

Represents the instantaneous vertical elongation rate in meters per year ($\frac{dH}{dt}$).

Step-by-Step Chain Rule

Click the steps below to animate the analytical differentiation pathway using the quotient and chain rules on the primary logistical model.

1

Setup Quotient/Reciprocal Rule

Rewrite the logistic function $H(t) = 12 \cdot u(t)^{-1}$ where $u(t) = 1 + 5e^{-0.22t}$ to apply power rule chain rule.

$$H(t) = 12 \left( 1 + 5e^{-0.22t} \right)^{-1}$$
2

Apply Outside-Inside Rule (Chain Rule)

Differentiate outer function $(-1 \cdot u^{-2})$ and multiply by derivative of inner function $\frac{d}{dt}(1 + 5e^{-0.22t})$.

$$\frac{dH}{dt} = 12 \cdot (-1) \left( 1 + 5e^{-0.22t} \right)^{-2} \cdot \frac{d}{dt} \left( 1 + 5e^{-0.22t} \right)$$
3

Differentiate exponential exponent

Differentiate the inner exponent: $\frac{d}{dt} (5e^{-0.22t}) = 5e^{-0.22t} \cdot (-0.22) = -1.1e^{-0.22t}$.

$$\frac{dH}{dt} = -12 \left( 1 + 5e^{-0.22t} \right)^{-2} \cdot \left( -1.1e^{-0.22t} \right)$$
4

Simplify and Final Elongation Derivative Model

Multiply scalars: $-12 \cdot (-1.1) = +13.2$. Return the negative exponent to the denominator for standard quotient form.

$$H'(t) = \frac{13.2e^{-0.22t}}{(1+5e^{-0.22t})^2}$$

Agrotechnology Syllabus Module

1. Sigmoid/Logistic Growth Curve

Biological growth is rarely infinite. Initial slow growth (lag phase) leads to rapid cell division (log phase), and eventually, resource depletion (light, soil nutrients, spaces) causes plateauing (stationary/asymptote phase). Logistic equations accurately model physical trunk expansion of oil palm until height asymptotes at roughly 12 meters due to osmotic pumping limits in standard environments.

2. The Bio-Meaning of the 1st Derivative

The first derivative, $\frac{dH}{dt}$, is the exact rate of trunk elongation. In early vegetative stages, velocity is low. When the palm reaches young maturity (4–10 years), the elongation speed peaks at almost 0.66 meters per year, which increases competitive forest floor canopy height, afterward tapering as resources shift to heavy seed bunch production.

3. Inflexion Points & Acceleration

The mathematical inflection point occurs where the growth acceleration $H''(t) = 0$. For our model: $$t_{inflection} = \frac{\ln(5)}{0.22} \approx 7.31 \text{ years}$$ This signals the exact biological transition moment from accelerating expansion to decelerating mature crop development, guiding nutrient formulation switches.

4. Harvesting & Replanting Limits

In Malaysia, physical height of $H \geq 10$ meters makes manual sickle harvesting with aluminum poles (egrek poles) hazardous, highly labor intensive, and inefficient. Calculated derivative-driven lifecycle metrics help estates predict when to schedule mechanical clearings, replanting, and chemical herbicide injections.
